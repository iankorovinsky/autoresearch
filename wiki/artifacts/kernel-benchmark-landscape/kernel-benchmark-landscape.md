# Kernel benchmark landscape

**Research snapshot: 2026-11-21.** 44 directly relevant benchmark entries, 45 related benchmarks and resources, and 6 unverified leads.

A broad, source-linked survey of benchmarks for models that write or optimize computational kernels, including GPU, CPU, TPU, NPU, mobile, and other accelerator targets. Operating-system kernel development is outside this scope.

- [Interactive report](kernel-benchmark-landscape.html): searchable tables with availability filters; download and open in a browser.
- [Structured research data](kernel-benchmark-landscape.json): all 95 entries with descriptions, availability notes, and source URLs.

## Scope and evidence status

This is a manual literature and release audit, not a guarantee of exhaustive coverage or a registered systematic review. Entries represent named families or variants, not independent datasets; several reuse or extend the same tasks. No benchmark was executed, and this document does not establish capabilities implemented by this repository.

Descriptions and task counts summarize the linked author/project sources. Availability labels are research judgments based on the inspected releases and licenses. Sources are live URLs, not frozen copies in the paper collection: verify the relevant release, source section, and license before using a consequential claim. Counts from different versions, hardware configurations, and task definitions are not directly comparable.

## Reading the data-availability column

- **Yes — open license:** released task assets or generators have a standard open code/data license or an explicit project declaration. Repository-level licensing is not a legal audit of every imported asset.
- **Public — restricted/custom:** downloadable assets with special terms; public access does not establish unrestricted reuse.
- **Public — license unverified:** assets are visible, but their reuse rights were not established.
- **Partial:** only part of the package was verified or could be obtained.
- **No — not released/private:** a private, commercial, or explicitly unreleased package.
- **Unverified release:** an accessible release could not be established.

“Data” includes prompts, reference implementations, shapes, input generators, and tests; it need not be a static dataset file. Public tests may still be hidden from the model during evaluation. Hardware access and external model weights are separate.

## Interpretation and important distinctions

KernelBench derivatives, production-trace suites, and hardware-specific ports overlap. Do not sum task counts into a unique-problem total. Speedup depends on the baseline, device, precision, workload shapes, compilation treatment, correctness conditions, and agent budget.

[ComputeEval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/compute-eval) and [SOL-ExecBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/SOL-ExecBench) publish data under NVIDIA evaluation-only terms even though their harnesses are Apache-2.0. [GPU MODE competition assets](https://github.com/gpu-mode/reference-kernels) and [KernelBook](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GPUMODE/KernelBook) had reciprocity conditions on model-improvement use at inspection. They are therefore not labeled as standard open-licensed data here.

THUNLP’s TritonBench evaluates kernel generation; Meta/PyTorch’s tritonbench is an operator-performance suite. Infatoshi’s KernelBench-v3 is a community project, not the official version number of Stanford’s KernelBench. Training corpora and validator studies are separated from direct generation evaluations.

## Search method and limitations

Searched papers, project sites, model system cards, GitHub, and Hugging Face using kernel-generation, kernel-optimization, CUDA, Triton, Pallas, NKI, Ascend, MUSA, Metal, scientific/HPC, translation, and validation terms. Followed citations and benchmark catalogs, then inspected author/project sources, release descriptions, and available license files. Code licensing was checked separately from external dataset terms where available.

Some OpenReview pages were blocked, some repositories or datasets were inaccessible, and some licenses could not be retrieved. These limits are recorded rather than treated as proof that no public release exists. Current repository counts are used when clearly documented; older paper snapshots can differ. Related traditional suites are included as workload sources, not as evidence that each is a standard model evaluation.
